# The rival, the scene and the car ladder (proposal, 2026-10-07)

Status: PROPOSAL, docs only. Story is Roy's to write; every name is a placeholder. Nothing here adds loan or penalty mechanics: the debt stays story-only.

Builds on `docs/story-bible.md` (main) and the narrative fabric proposal (PR #175). Replaces the "Vance Ledger, paranoid kingpin / Kess enforcer" draft from 2026-10-07, which Roy was not sold on.

Roy's calls this follows:
- The debt holder (Pike) is **a mechanic, not a place**. The Pike Lending storefront became a liquor store.
- The garage has day and night: **day you work, night you go out**, the sun rising sends you back to the shop.
- Prologue car weaker than today's coupe. Open to building or remodelling cars for a bigger spread of tiers.
- Story must fit the garage, Dave, racing and mechanic world.

## One line

**In Harlow Bay, crews pick their mechanic by whose cars win at night.** Racing is the shop's advertising. The rival is the other shop.

## Steelman and premortem

- Steelman: making the antagonist a rival mechanic (Roy's own "Pike is a mechanic") means every system already tells the story. Each race is your build against his build, each customer car in the day shift is a customer you took from him, and the debt is between two shops, not a bank. Nobody needs to be a cartoon villain.
- Premortem, it failed because:
  1. "Rival mechanic" felt small next to street-racing kingpins. Mitigation: Pike builds the cars for every rival crew, so his name is on everything you race. He is the scene's money, not one guy with a wrench.
  2. Too many names (Walt, Pike, Kess, Dave, Ferris, Okafor, NULL, 4 crew, 4 crew leaders, 5 kings). Mitigation: cut Ledger, give each mentor a job in the shop economy, and keep crew leaders unnamed until Roy writes them.
  3. The backstory only lives in a text log nobody reads. Mitigation: Dave tells it on air in short "back when" bits, one per night, so it arrives while driving.
- Falsification: if a playtester can't say who Pike is after Act 1, he is too offscreen. Fix by putting his sticker on more Act 1 opponents, not by adding cutscenes.

## How the real scene works (what this borrows)

These are the real-world patterns the story leans on, so it feels lived-in rather than invented:

| Real pattern | Where it shows up here |
|---|---|
| Tuning shops made their names by proving builds on public roads at night (Japan's Mid Night Club era on the Wangan; shop owners like Top Secret's Smokey Nagata became legends that way) | Shops advertise by winning street races. Pike's sticker on a winning car is his business |
| A shop can claim a car whose owner abandons it with an unpaid repair bill (mechanic's lien, rules vary by state) | Where the starter car and some later cars come from |
| Tow drivers steer wrecks to the body shops that pay them referral fees; parts of that trade got organised and dirty (Ontario "tow truck wars", US kickback cases) | Ferris decides which shop gets every wreck in town. Pike pays him; you have to earn him |
| Shops poach each other's technicians with better pay | Mid-game crew drama: Pike hires one of your crew |
| Pirate radio and scanner chatter as the scene's news | Dave's station is the scoreboard and the history book |

## The cast

### Pike (rival mechanic, debt holder, final opponent)

- **Who:** Walt's old partner. Twenty-odd years ago the shop was **Dunmore & Pike**. Pike was the better builder and the louder one.
- **The split:** Pike wanted the crews, the money and the fast work; Walt wanted to fix the neighbourhood's cars properly. Pike left and took the racing customers with him. His new place, **Pike Speed** (placeholder), is clean, bright and expensive, and builds the cars every rival crew drives.
- **The debt:** a few bad years later Walt needed money for the lift and the roof. Pike lent it "as a friend", on paper, against the building. $60k due at season's end. Pike doesn't want the money; he wants the Dunmore lot and the name, so the town has one shop.
- **Not a villain:** he is good at his job, he thinks Walt is stubborn and sentimental, and he is mostly right about the business. What he does wrong is the setup (the Turn).
- **In play:** you rarely see him. You see his sticker on rival cars, his tow fees on Ferris's truck, his name in Dave's gossip, and his shop's lights from the freeway. He shows up in person three times: the prologue (after the race), the Turn, the finale.

### Kess (Pike's driver)

Recommended: keep her, but rewrite her.
- **Who:** Pike's house driver. She tests and races his builds, and she is the best driver in town in a car she didn't build. She grew up on the same strip as Juno (they ran launches together as kids), so the crew knows her.
- **Prologue:** she is the driver who beats you, in a Pike build, while Pike tells everyone watching that Dunmore Auto is finished.
- **Act 2:** she isn't a boss. She turns up at meets, races you when she feels like it, and sometimes wins. She is a rival with respect, the measuring stick.
- **The Turn:** she learns the prologue was arranged (Pike set the race up and put out the word to make the shop look washed up so customers leave before the debt is due). She doesn't switch sides; she is just done being used.
- **Finale:** she drives Pike's best build against yours. If you win, she walks into Dunmore Auto the next day and asks what you'd do to her car. That's the ending's last beat.

### Vance Ledger

Recommended: **retire him.** Pike plus Kess cover the debt, the rival shop and the rival driver, and a third antagonist splits the focus. If Roy likes the name, two alternatives: Ledger becomes Pike's first name, or the Ironbridge crew leader (an Act 2 boss only).

### Walt Dunmore (shop owner)

- Unchanged: tired and honest, no father figure. Never tells you to race. He just leaves the keys on the bench.
- Gets the backstory weight: he and Pike were friends. He won't talk about it; Dave will.

### Dave (Graveyard TV)

- **Ties:** he has broadcast from the shop's back room since the Dunmore & Pike days. He is the only one who'll tell the story of the split, and he does it in small bits on air.
- **Three jobs on air:**
  1. **Scoreboard:** who won tonight and whose build it was. "Pike Speed, four wins this week. Dunmore Auto, one. Ouch." Your reputation is told through Dave.
  2. **History:** a "back when" segment, one short piece per night, telling the Walt/Pike story out of order, so the backstory arrives while you drive.
  3. **Gossip and tips:** what the crews are doing, where the cops are sitting, who brought a new car.
- **Turn night:** the station goes quiet (from PR #175). New: because Pike's people tipped off the city about the pirate signal. Dave is back the next night, angrier.
- The broken coffee machine is from the Dunmore & Pike days. Nobody has fixed it in twenty years. Running joke, and the last job you do in the ending.

### Mentors, now part of the shop economy

| Mentor | Job in the world | How it ties to the rivalry |
|---|---|---|
| Ferris (tow driver) | Decides which shop gets every wreck and breakdown in town | Pike pays him a referral fee. Earning Ferris's trust (favours, tip-runs) is what makes wrecks start coming to Dunmore. Also the roadside-call option in PR #175 |
| Okafor (ex-factory tester) | Runs the only honest dyno in town | Neutral. His dyno sheets are the proof, in numbers, that your build beats Pike's. He's the one who notices Pike's numbers don't add up (Turn clue) |
| NULL (online tuner) | Sells tunes, never appears | Optional mystery: hint that NULL used to work for Pike. Roy's call, can stay unexplained |

### The crew and the rival crews

- **Crew (unchanged names):** Juno, Teo, Pilar, Moose. New ties: Juno knows Kess from the launch strip; Teo is the one Pike poaches (better pay, clean shop) in Act 2, and wins back after seeing how Pike cuts corners; Moose was Walt's last employee before the money ran out.
- **Rival crews are Pike's customers.** Ironbridge, Kasumi Run, Gruppe 9, Diesel Row all drive Pike builds at the start. Beating a crew's top driver in Act 2 doesn't just take a district: **that crew starts bringing its cars to Dunmore.** Their cars become day-shift customer jobs. Win at night, the work comes in the day.
- This answers PR #175's question 5 without Ledger: each Act 2 boss is a crew leader, and the final race is Pike's build with Kess driving.

## The spine, re-told

| Beat | Night (streets) | Day (garage) | Dave on air |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Kess beats you in a Pike build; Pike mocks Dunmore Auto in front of the meet | Walt hands you the old car under the tarp (see the ladder below) | First broadcast: deadpan report of the loss |
| Act 1 | First wins against small crews; Ferris's first favour | First service brings the car back to stock; first customer cars; Juno and Moose join | Scoreboard starts; first "back when" bit |
| Act 2 | Beat each crew's top driver; their district and their business switch to you | Customer jobs from the switched crews; scrapyard swaps; Teo poached, then won back | Pike's win count drops on air; Kess races you when she likes |
| The Turn | Station goes dark; Pike's crews outside the liquor store | Okafor's dyno sheet and Kess's word: the prologue was arranged | Dave back the next night, angrier, tells the full split |
| Act 3 | The five kings; the season invitational | Engine rebuild; the top build | Dave calls the finale race live |
| Ending | Your build vs Pike's, Kess driving. Win: the note is torn up. Lose: shop sold, crew survives | Kess walks in with her car; you fix the coffee machine | Last broadcast |

## The car ladder (more cars, clearer tiers)

Roy wants a bigger spread of tiers and is fine with remodelling. The story gives every car a reason to reach the player, so new cars aren't just a dealer list.

### Where cars come from (no dealership)

| Source | Story reason | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Abandoned in the shop | Owner never paid the bill; the shop can claim it | The starter car |
| Crew cars | Crew members join with their own cars | Juno's hatch, Pilar's roadster |
| Scrapyard rebuilds | Wrecks Ferris tows in that nobody claims | Mid-tier project cars |
| Won from a crew | A crew leader puts a car up after losing (story events only) | One per rival crew |
| Customer test drives | You drive a customer's car around the block after a job | Teaser for higher tiers, not owned |

### Tiers (ties to PR #175's power tiers)

| Tier | Feel | Existing cars | Suggested new cars |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 Beater | Tired, slow, honest | none | **Old shop car (starter)** |
| T1 Street | Light, cheap, fun | P4 kei roadster, P2 hot hatch | A compact wagon or small van (work car with a sleeper tune) |
| T2 Club | Real performance, needs care | P3 tuner sedan, P6 crossover | A mid-80s rear-drive coupe (drift class) |
| T3 District | Each crew's style at its best | P1 sports coupe, P5 muscle sedan | One car per rival crew style: a truck for Diesel Row, a euro sedan for Gruppe 9 |
| T4 Kings | Top of the scene | none | A mid-engine car and a homologation rally car (kings' cars, won late) |

That is 6 existing plus about 7 new player cars, about 13 total, all original designs in the existing silhouette rules. Each new car is its own Fable design pass and Roy sign-off, one at a time.
